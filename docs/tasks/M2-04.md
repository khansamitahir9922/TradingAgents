## Goal
Define how a rule strategy states **what it needs** (required sources, Law 9; features) and **what it returns**: a direction and a strength, never a probability. Strategies are deterministic and versioned.

## Rules
1. **Determinism:** the same snapshot gives the same signal, with no hidden state, no randomness and no clock reads.
2. **Strength** is in [0, 1], and its meaning is documented per strategy. It's never labelled or used as a probability. `probability_up` exists only for calibrated models (M9).
3. **Warm-up gives `None`**, never a guessed direction.
4. **A strategy reads only declared features.** Reading another key raises, which a test enforces through a guarded snapshot view.
5. Both the pipeline and concrete strategies use `declared_snapshot` before evaluating. Declared keys must match snapshot definitions, parameters, input basis and versions. StrategySignal validates a finite float strength (booleans and Decimal refused), non-empty identity/version and permitted direction. The output's feature keys enumerate the actual declared inputs.

## Implementation choices
**Resolved:** strategies are pure functions of the snapshot. DataHealth checking and abstention belong to the policy (M2-06), so Law 9 has one implementation.
**Unresolved:** none.

## Acceptance tests
- A conforming dummy strategy passes a contract test; strength outside [0, 1] raises.
- Reading an undeclared feature raises.
- Warm-up gives `None`.
- The same snapshot gives an identical signal across calls.

## Out of scope
The first strategies (M2-05); ML models (M9).
