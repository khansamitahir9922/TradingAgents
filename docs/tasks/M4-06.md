## Goal
A broker behind the broker port, with its own durable state, and a **causal** daily fill model. It never uses a price before the order existed, never claims an intraday price touch happened at the open, and never invents a fill from missing data. Each fill carries the provenance needed to reconstruct it, and there is a contract suite that later adapters must pass (Law 20; Part K M4; Part J contract tests; D25, D26).

## Contracts
```python
class SimulatedBroker(BrokerPort):   # M4-03 port
    def __init__(self, db, *, knowledge: Knowledge, calendar, source: str, fees: FeeSchedule): ...
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class FeeSchedule: version: str; commission_per_order: Decimal; slippage_bps: Decimal   # D25: 0, 5

# portfolio/reference.py, on the Knowledge port (V1)
def first_known_closed(self, source: str, instrument_id: str, session: date, *, not_after: datetime,
                       calendar=None) -> tuple[datetime, Candle] | None:
    """The earliest knowledge time k <= not_after at which latest_closed(session, at=k) is not None, and that bar.
    k is a time at which some revision became known: max(available_from, received_at) for StoreKnowledge, and
    max(available_from, delivered) for ReplayKnowledge (delivered = available_from + latency, with equal-time
    sequence). The bar is the revision latest_closed returns at k, never one learned later."""
```

## Rules
1. **Its own state.** The tables in schema `sim_broker` are written only by `SimulatedBroker`. The Core Engine and the reporter never read them (layering plus a SQL grep test). They see only what the worker records in the order store.
2. **Four separate times** (B5):
   - **submitted_at**, the broker's receipt time. The gate guarantees it is before the target session's open (M4-05 rule 1.7). The broker also refuses a submit at or after the open with REJECTED "daily model: orders must rest from the open".
   - **executed_at**, when the modelled execution happened: the session open, or `INTRADAY_UNKNOWN` (rule 3).
   - **delivered_at**, when the broker makes the message available: the session's **decision moment** (rule 6), the first time its CLOSED daily bar is actually **known**, through both availability and delivery. That is never before the session's close, and never before the knowledge it depends on.
   - **recorded_at**, when the worker ingests it (M4-07).
3. **The daily fill model** (D25), for an order resting from the target session's open, using the session's raw daily bar as returned by `first_known_closed` at the decision moment:
   - **BUY:** open ≤ limit fills at the open, with `executed_at` = the open.
     - Else, if low ≤ limit, it fills at the limit, with `executed_at` = **the session close and `timing = INTRADAY_UNKNOWN`**. The bar can't say when the touch happened, so it is never stamped at the open.
     - Otherwise there is no fill.
   - **SELL** mirrors this, with high ≥ limit.
   - **Slippage** of `slippage_bps` moves the price against the order, then it is capped at the limit. It is rounded to the cent with an explicit rounding mode, in a local decimal context.
   - The full quantity always fills (D19). The fee is `commission_per_order`.
4. **Provenance on every fill** (B5). It is canonical JSON with: the bar's provider, instrument, timeframe, start, revision and import id; the model version; the base price (open or limit); the slippage bps; the price after slippage and after the cap; the fee schedule version; and the timing basis. The worker stores it with the fill (M4-00 `Fill.provenance`), so the reporter needs nothing from the broker's private state.
5. **Cancels: receipt is separate from outcome** (R1). The broker persists every cancel with its **receipt time `c`**, which is the first receipt; a repeated cancel keeps the original `c`. It never reads a price it can't yet know:
   - **`c` before the open:** conclusive at once, because the order never rests and no price is needed. `cancel` returns CANCELLED, and the CANCELLED message is delivered at `c`.
   - **`c` at or after the open and strictly before the decision moment `D`:** `cancel` returns **PENDING** ("received; outcome decided when the session's bar is known"). Nothing is delivered yet. `lookup` reports `CANCEL_RECEIVED` with `c`. The worker keeps the order CANCEL_PENDING and the request SENT (M4-07 rule 3). Receiving a cancel never closes the request.
   - **`c` at or after `D`** (V2): too late to affect anything. The outcome was fixed at `D` and is already materialized (rule 7), so `cancel` returns it conclusively: **TOO_LATE** for a filled or expired order, CANCELLED for an order already cancelled. Nothing pending is created, and no earlier message changes. **Tie rule:** `c == D` is too late.
   - **Resolution at `D`**, with each persisted `c < D` (the eligible interval is from the open to `D`, strictly):
     - the open satisfied the limit: the open fill stands, a FILL is delivered, and a CANCELLED message isn't sent;
     - only an intraday touch could fill: **the cancel wins**, because the touch time is unknown (the conservative assumption, recorded in provenance), and CANCELLED is delivered;
     - neither: CANCELLED is delivered.
6. **The decision moment, fixed by knowledge, not by polling** (R1, V1). Each session's outcome (fill, expiry, or the resolution of a pending cancel) is decided once, at the session's **decision moment `D`**:
   - `D` = the `k` from `first_known_closed(source, instrument, session, not_after=cutoff)`. That is the first time a CLOSED revision was actually **known**, with availability and delivery (replay latency) both counted and equal-time sequence respected. The bar used is the revision known at `D`, never the newest one at polling time.
   - It is independent of when, or how often, the broker is polled. A correction known only later never changes it. All of that session's messages carry `delivered_at = D`.
7. **Materialize before answering** (V2). Before handling **any** call at time `t` (`submit`, `cancel`, `lookup`, `messages`), the broker first materializes every outcome whose `D ≤ t`, using only the cancels received before each `D`. A late first poll therefore sees exactly what an early poll would have, with the same messages, receipt times and ids, and a cancel arriving after `D` meets an outcome that is already fixed.
   - A DAY order expires at its session close whatever happens.
   - **Missing data:** if no CLOSED bar is **known** (in the V1 sense) by the **next regular session's open** (the cutoff), `D` is the cutoff. A revision that is available before the cutoff but delivered after it counts as missing. An order without a pending cancel is EXPIRED with "no market data for the session; no fill modelled". An order with a pending cancel is CANCELLED with the same reason. No fill is invented. A bar that arrives later changes nothing.
   - A cancel received at or after the cutoff meets an EXPIRED outcome and gets TOO_LATE; it never turns an earlier EXPIRED into CANCELLED (V2).
   - Replays therefore agree, whatever the polling cadence.
8. **Submit is idempotent** by `client_order_id`: a repeat returns the existing order. Invalid orders (not LIMIT/DAY, a non-whole quantity, an unknown instrument, submitted at or after the open) are REJECTED with a reason. **Lookup** returns the current report (including `CANCEL_RECEIVED` and `c`), or `None`. **Messages** are durable, with stable `message_id`s, ordered by `(delivered_at, message_id)`, and include only those with `delivered_at ≤ at`.
9. **Determinism.** No randomness and no wall clock. The same orders, bars, settings and times give the same messages and ids.
10. **The contract suite** (Part J) takes a `BrokerPort` factory and covers: submit/lookup, idempotent submit, cancel before and after a fill, no record, duplicate message delivery, and delivery-time filtering. The simulator and the test double both pass it. M7's adapter must pass it too.

## Acceptance tests
- **The fill table, hand-computed:** BUY and SELL, open inside the limit (stamped at the open); intraday touch only (stamped at the close, `INTRADAY_UNKNOWN`, never at the open); never reached; slippage capped at the limit; cent rounding shown.
- **Late submission:** a submit at or after the open is REJECTED, and the gate also prevents it.
- **Cancel before delivery (R1):** a BUY limit 100 submitted before the open, cancelled at 10:00. Two alternative future bars: (a) open 90; (b) open 110, low 95. Before either bar is known, both give **the same** PENDING response and the same `lookup`. After delivery: (a) a FILL at the open; (b) CANCELLED, with the assumption recorded. A cancel before the open: CANCELLED at once, with no price read.
- **Retries and restart:** a repeated cancel keeps the original `c`; after a broker restart (a fresh `SimulatedBroker` on the same tables), resolution still uses the original `c`.
- **The decision moment (R1, V1):** zero and nonzero replay latency (available 20:00, delivered 20:10, so `D` = 20:10 and no message is delivered at 20:00); a revision available before the cutoff but delivered after it (missing, so EXPIRED at the cutoff); a later correction never used; early versus late polling over identical delivery histories gives identical messages and ids. No message precedes the knowledge that determines it.
- **Late cancels (V2):** BUY limit 100, open 110, low 95, bar known at 16:00. A cancel at 15:59 gives PENDING and then CANCELLED at 16:00. A cancel **at** 16:00 or at 16:30 gives TOO_LATE, with the intraday fill delivered at 16:00, identically whether polling is frequent or the first poll is at 17:00. A cancel after the missing-data cutoff gives TOO_LATE, and EXPIRED stands. Retries and restarts keep the first receipt time.
- `first_known_closed` for StoreKnowledge and ReplayKnowledge: FORMING before CLOSED, equal-time sequence, and `not_after` bounds.
- **Missing bar:** no bar by the cutoff gives EXPIRED "no market data", or CANCELLED with that reason if a cancel was pending; no fill; a bar arriving later changes nothing.
- Expiry at a regular close and at a half-day close; duplicate delivery; provenance round-trips exactly; the contract suite passes.

## Out of scope
Partial fills (only in the test double, D19), market orders, intraday bars, paper adapters (M7).
